## This week's Journal Entries

### Journal Entry, 2025-12-08

My hands are still shaking a little. I had to report a student today — Jia-Hui, one of my brightest — for cheating on the midterm exam. She copied answers from the student beside her. I saw it clearly, and worse, she saw me see it.

The look on her face. Like I'd betrayed her somehow, when she was the one breaking the rules. I followed protocol exactly: documented the incident, submitted the report to the academic integrity office, informed her homeroom teacher. Everything by the book. But she's been crying in the bathroom all afternoon, and three of her friends gave me cold looks in the hallway.

The thing that keeps running through my head is what Principal Wu said when he reviewed my report: "Good catch, Teacher Chen. We appreciate your vigilance." Vigilance. Like I'm some kind of guard. I don't want to be the person students are afraid of. I want them to trust me. But if I'd looked away, if I'd pretended not to notice — wouldn't that be worse? Wouldn't that be telling Jia-Hui that rules don't matter, that integrity is flexible?

My mother would say I did right. "A teacher who ignores cheating isn't a teacher," she'd say. She taught chemistry for thirty years and never once let a student slide. I'm trying to believe that following the proper procedure was the correct choice. I just wish it didn't feel so much like I've lost something.

*Their reply to a follow-up question:* Her trust, I suppose. Or maybe the version of myself that I prefer — the approachable teacher, the one students come to with their problems. Now I'm the one who reported Jia-Hui to administration. There's no taking that back. She'll always remember me as the teacher who got her in trouble, not the one who stayed after school to help her understand quadratic equations. I know I did what was expected of me. I just wonder sometimes if being the person everyone expects me to be means giving up other things I want to be.

Saturday afternoon and I'm sitting in my apartment with a cup of cold tea I forgot to drink, thinking about the faculty dinner next Friday. I keep thinking about it. I haven't signed up because it conflicts with my parents' usual visiting time. They would understand if I explained. They would say "of course, go to your class." But my mother's face would do that thing it does when she's disappointed but trying not to show it. And my father would eat dinner in silence the way he does when something's bothering him.

Is it ridiculous that I can't take a ceramics class because of a look on my mother's face? It sounds ridiculous when I write it out. I'll probably end up watching YouTube videos about ceramics instead. It's not the same, but it's something.
